  Information architect and content strategist. Four enterprise case studies on data discovery,
  deployment compliance, and scenario modeling, each starting from what was asked and what the
  problem actually was.

hero:
  headline:
    - Structure before aesthetics.
    - Diagnosis before design.
  lead: The ask is rarely the need.
  examples:
    - A site for finding reports was really a *metadata* problem.
    - A platform to stop bad deployments was really a *visibility* problem.
    - A stalled project to model scenarios was really a *vocabulary* problem.
  closing: I find the *problem* first, then design the processes, tools, and content needed to solve it.

approach:
  Diagnose before designing: >-
    Every project starts with surfacing misaligned assumptions. Shared vocabulary doesn’t mean
    shared meaning, and the reference interview trained me to find the gap between what people ask
    for and what the problem is. Case study 03 is that under a deadline: a six-month stall,
    resolved in five days.
  Define the objects before the interface: >-
    Domain modeling, taxonomy design, and metadata standards come before wireframes. The structure
    is the decision.
  Cross-functional by default: >-
    Embedded with dev, business, and governance stakeholders rather than handed off to them.
    Influence through clarity of craft, not organizational hierarchy.
  Documentation that survives turnover: >-
    Systems designed so the logic is clear enough that people use the docs to train the next person.
